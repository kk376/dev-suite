# Humanizer

A checklist-driven rewrite pass that strips out the stylistic tells that make
text read as AI-generated, based on patterns Wikipedia editors catalogued from
thousands of real AI-written submissions.

## How to use this

1. Take the draft (yours or the user's).
2. Run it against the **Pattern Checklist** below, flagging every match.
3. Rewrite — don't just delete the flagged words. Deleting "furthermore" and
   leaving everything else the same doesn't fix the underlying sameyness; the
   sentence rhythm and structure need to actually vary.
4. Do one final read as if you're a skeptical human editor: does any sentence
   sound like it's performing "sounding smart" rather than just saying the
   thing?

Important: these are *signals in combination*, not a banned-word list. A single
em dash or a single triplet isn't proof of anything — humans use both. The tell
is when a piece leans on the same few crutches repeatedly. Don't overcorrect
into stiff, paranoid prose that avoids all normal words just to dodge the list;
that reads as artificial in its own way.

## Pattern Checklist

### Sentence-level tics

- **Em dash overuse** — especially em dashes used for punchy emphasis where a
  comma, colon, or period would do. AI tends to space them oddly and reach for
  them constantly. Fix: replace most with a comma, period, or parenthetical;
  keep at most one per few paragraphs if it's genuinely the best tool.
- **Negative parallelism / contrast-reframe** — "It's not X, it's Y," "not
  just X, but Y," "no X, no Y, just Z." Manufactures fake depth. Fix: just
  state the point directly.
- **Rule of three** — forcing lists, adjectives, or examples into groups of
  exactly three ("innovative, transformative, and groundbreaking"). Fix: use
  however many items actually fit — one, two, four, whatever's true.
- **Avoidance of plain "is/are"** — swapping simple copula verbs for fancier
  constructions to sound sophisticated. Fix: let simple sentences be simple.

### Padding and fake depth

- **Editorializing filler** — "it's important to note," "it's worth
  remembering," "no discussion would be complete without." Fix: cut it; state
  the fact itself.
- **Present-participle tack-ons** — sentences that end with a dangling "-ing"
  clause manufacturing significance: "...highlighting its role in,"
  "...underscoring the importance of." Fix: cut, or make it its own real
  sentence with substance.
- **Vague, unnamed attribution** — "some critics argue," "experts say,"
  "studies show" with no actual named source. Fix: name the source or drop
  the claim.
- **Puffed-up significance** — connecting something small to a Big Broader
  Theme it doesn't need. Fix: let small things stay small.

### Structure and formatting

- **Bolded-lead-in bullets** — "**Term:** sentence that just restates the bold
  term." Fix: either cut the bold lead-in or make the sentence add real
  information the header doesn't already say.
- **Excessive boldface / title-case headers / emoji in headings** —
  mechanical-feeling emphasis. Fix: bold only what's genuinely load-bearing;
  skip decorative emoji.
- **Rigid formulaic section structure** — always the same shape (intro → three
  sections → "Challenges" → "Future Outlook" → conclusion) regardless of what
  the content actually needs. Fix: let structure follow the content, not a
  template.
- **Repetitive wrap-up phrases** — "In summary," "Overall," "In conclusion"
  used reflexively. Fix: either end on the actual point or cut the sign-post
  entirely.

### Tone

- **Generic exaggerated positivity** — "innovative," "transformative,"
  "groundbreaking," "game-changing" stacked up without specifics backing them.
  Fix: replace with the concrete detail that would justify the adjective, or
  cut the adjective.
- **Promotional/travel-brochure warmth** — admiring tone where plain
  description would do. Fix: describe, don't sell.
- **Leftover assistant-speak** — "I hope this helps!", "let me know if you
  need anything else," "I hope this message finds you well" — showing up in
  content that isn't actually a message to the reader. Fix: delete; it doesn't
  belong outside a direct reply.
- **Overused hedges/transitions** — "furthermore," "however," "moreover,"
  "additionally" doing all the connective work instead of natural flow. Fix:
  vary connectors or cut them — often the sentences link fine without one.

## Quick self-check before finalizing

Ask: if I read this out loud to someone, does any line sound like it's
reciting a template rather than talking? Would a human editor, skimming fast,
flag any paragraph as "trying too hard to sound authoritative"? If yes, rewrite
that line in the plainest possible words and start again from there.
